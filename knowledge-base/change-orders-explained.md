# What is a Change Order?

In construction, a change order is a formal document that details an amendment to the original construction contract. It represents an agreement between the client and the contractor to alter the scope of work, the project cost, or the completion schedule.

Change orders are a normal part of the construction process and can arise for many reasons:

* **Client Requests:** The property owner decides to add a feature or change a material.
* **Unforeseen Conditions:** Workers discover unexpected issues on-site, like poor soil conditions or hidden structural problems, that require additional work.
* **Design Errors:** An error or omission in the original architectural plans is discovered.
* **Material Substitutions:** A specified material becomes unavailable, requiring a different, often more expensive, alternative.

A proper change order is a mini-contract in itself. It should clearly describe the change, the new cost (or credit), and any adjustment to the project timeline. Meticulously tracking change orders is essential for FP&A, as they can significantly impact a project's budget and profitability.
