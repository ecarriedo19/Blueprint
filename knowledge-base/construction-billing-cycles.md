# Understanding Construction Billing Cycles

Unlike traditional businesses that sell a product and get paid immediately, construction billing is a complex process tied to project progress. The most common method is **progress billing**.

With progress billing, a contractor invoices the client at regular intervals (usually monthly) based on the percentage of work completed. This process relies on a crucial document called a **Schedule of Values**.

A Schedule of Values is a detailed list of every work item on a project, from site prep to final finishes, along with its cost. At the end of each billing cycle, the contractor assesses the percentage of completion for each line item to calculate the amount due.

Key terms associated with construction billing include:

* **Application for Payment:** The formal invoice submitted by the contractor.
* **Retainage:** A percentage of the payment (often 5-10%) that the client holds back until the project is fully completed to their satisfaction. This acts as a form of insurance.
* **Lien Waiver:** A document signed by the contractor or subcontractor, waiving their right to place a lien (a legal claim) on the property once they have been paid for their work.

Managing this cycle effectively is critical for maintaining healthy cash flow throughout a long-term construction project.
